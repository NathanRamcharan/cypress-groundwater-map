Evidence for the Thesis

This page lays out the three data layers side by side, on the same timeline, to show what actually overlaps — and what doesn't.
WORKING THESIS From the early 1900s to the present day, the Cypress area has not had subsidence stations, wells, and flooding data coexist to provide enough proof that the sinking of the ground causes flooding, with too many outside factors becoming involved, such as rainfall intensity and the addition of pavements.

1. Coverage over time — where the three layers do and don't overlap

Wells were well-monitored pre-1985, but subsidence and flood-claims data barely existed yet. The 2016–17 flood spike happened while subsidence stations were running well, but wells were almost entirely dark. The two eras of strongest data never line up.

2. FEMA flood insurance claims, 1978–2024 — the actual spike

Real claims data for both Cypress zip codes (77429, 77433), combined by year. 2016 and 2017 are not a small bump — they are 10 to 50 times every other year in the record. This is the single largest signal in the entire dataset, and it lands squarely in the years wells were dark. Note: the 77429 API query hit its 1,000-record cap, so its later years may be undercounted; 77433 is complete.

3. Differential subsidence — the mechanism the thesis depends on

P018 and AULT sit only a few miles apart but move at different paces: P018 has been sinking since 2000 and is now decelerating; AULT only started being tracked in 2015 and is accelerating. Both landed at a similar 2025 rate (~1.9 cm/yr) by very different paths — real evidence subsidence is not uniform across Cypress, which is the condition under which subsidence could plausibly worsen flooding (per the Talbott/HGSD debate in the literature review). But neither station has a matched well or flood-claims record at that same specific point, so the link stops at correlation.

4. What this does and doesn't prove

Supported by data: groundwater is declining at every tracked well; subsidence is real, ongoing, and differential by location; flood losses spiked severely in 2016–2017.

Not supported by data: that subsidence specifically — as opposed to rainfall intensity, new impervious surface from development, or aging drainage infrastructure — caused the 2016–2017 flood spike. No dataset here isolates subsidence's contribution from those other factors.

5. Field documentation — site visit, Sept 2026

Primary-source photos taken in person, not from a database, at all three planned stops.
Stop 1 — Historical well site. Site of wells 65-03-204 and 65-03-206 (monitored 1931/1957–1985). Correction to an earlier assumption in this project — this parcel is commercial/business land, not a residential subdivision as originally guessed. Even the "what replaced the monitoring" story needed to be verified on the ground, not assumed from a map.
Stop 2 — Cypress Park (near Gage 1165, Cypress Creek @ Eldridge Pkwy N.). Wide, calm lake within the park, crossed by a wooden pedestrian bridge. Checklist: water brownish, mostly still; a small channel actively feeding into the lake was visible; litter present (bottle, shoe) near the waterline; wildlife present (egret).
Stop 3 — Russ Poppe Family Park (near Gage 1160, Cypress Creek @ Grant Road). Correction to the planned route: the water here is a wooded pond adjacent to the park, not a paved trail directly along Cypress Creek as assumed when this stop was planned. Water is noticeably darker/more tannic (brownish-green) than at Cypress Park, banks are natural and wooded rather than mowed, with a sandy edge visible on the far shore.
Checked against satellite mapping: Russ Poppe Family Park actually has two separate water features — Cypress Creek itself (a winding channel along the park's edge) and two distinct rounded ponds inside the park boundary. Based on the contained, oval shape and lack of visible current in these photos, this is most likely one of the park's ponds, not Cypress Creek's main channel — so it should not be cited as a direct stand-in for gage 1160's readings. It's still valid documentation of the park and surrounding water conditions, just not the creek channel itself.
